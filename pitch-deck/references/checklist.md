# Deck checklist

Use this for the checklist part of a review, or hand it to a founder to self-check. Each item notes where it comes from so you can explain why it matters.

## The story

- [ ] A stranger can repeat what the company does after the first slide (Seibel)
- [ ] The deck opens with, or quickly reaches, the thesis: the 3–5 things an investor must believe (Hoffman)
- [ ] The strongest proof (traction, insight or team) appears in the first three slides
- [ ] The biggest objection is answered early, not left for Q&A (Hoffman)
- [ ] "Why now" names a specific change, with a date or number (Sequoia, DocSend)
- [ ] The last slide is the thesis or vision, not "Questions?" (Hoffman)

## The evidence

- [ ] Traction is real usage or revenue, labelled honestly; pilots are called pilots (Casado)
- [ ] Market size is bottom-up: number of customers × what they pay (Seibel)
- [ ] The competition slide names real alternatives and says why they win (Hoffman, DocSend)
- [ ] One business model, stated in one sentence (Seibel, Hoffman)
- [ ] The team slide explains why these people for this problem (Casado, Seibel)
- [ ] The ask says how much and which milestones it reaches
- [ ] Every number matches the data room and passes the blink test (Moore, Hoffman)
- [ ] Main risks are named, with a plan for each (Hoffman)

## The slides

- [ ] One idea per slide (Hale)
- [ ] Every title states its takeaway (Hale)
- [ ] No text under ~24–30 pt on the presented version (Hale, Kawasaki)
- [ ] Charts have a headline stating the conclusion (Hale)
- [ ] Product shown as a clean mockup or simplified steps, not a busy screenshot (Hale)
- [ ] No jargon, buzzwords, "very", or vague words like "platform" (Seibel, Hoffman)
- [ ] No table of contents, transitions or animations (DocSend, Hale)
- [ ] There's a sparse version to present and a self-explanatory version to send
- [ ] Appendix covers the hard questions: financials, cohorts, unit economics (Hoffman)

## Mistakes that get decks passed on

| Mistake | Why it hurts |
| --- | --- |
| Can't say what you do in two sentences | The investor can't repeat it to partners, so it goes nowhere |
| Overclaiming traction | Falls apart in diligence and takes credibility with it |
| Top-down TAM | Signals you don't know your customer |
| "No competitors" | Means no market, or you haven't looked |
| Several business models | Reads as lack of focus |
| Jargon and buzzwords | Hides what you actually do |
| Crowded slides, small text | Nobody absorbs them in a 3-minute skim |
| Charts without a stated point | The reader has to decode it, and won't |
| Team slide first | Spends the best attention on the least differentiated slide |
| Hiding the big risk | Investors find it and assume you missed or hid it |
| Ending on "Questions?" | Wastes the last impression |
| Table of contents | No funded deck in DocSend's data had one |
